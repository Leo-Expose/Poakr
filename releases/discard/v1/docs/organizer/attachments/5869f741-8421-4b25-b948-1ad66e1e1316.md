Pokerbots
Rulebook
Submission deadline: 15 November 2026, 23:59 IST
Values marked (provisional) may be adjusted after benchmarking; any change is announced
to all teams before the submission deadline.
1 Scope
1.1 Pokerbots is an online competition in which a team’s Python bot plays a custom poker
variant against other teams’ bots on an automated server.
1.2 The game uses virtual chips only. There is no entry fee and no real-money play.
1.3 These rules apply to all teams. The Problem Statement and the Protocol Specification,
released by the organisers before the submission deadline, form part of this rulebook. If they
conflict with this rulebook, this rulebook prevails, except for the variant rules (Section 4),
where the Problem Statement prevails.
1.4 Terms used: a hand is one deal played by two bots; a duplicate deal is two hands with the
same cards swapped between seats; a match is a series of duplicate deals between two bots;
the engine is the organisers’ match software.
2 Eligibility
2.1 Participation is open to participants from any college or city.
2.2 Each team has exactly two members. Teams of one, or of three or more, are not accepted.
2.3 A person may be a member of only one team.
3 Registration
3.1 Registration requires a unique team name and, for both members, name, email, college and
city.
3.2 Both members must consent to these rules at registration.
3.3 Email addresses are verified. A registration with an unverified email is not valid.
3.4 Member 1 is the team’s point of contact and receives all official communication.
4 The Game
4.1 The game is Discard Hold’em, a heads-up Texas Hold’em variant with two changed
mechanics. Full rules and worked examples are in the Problem Statement.
4.2 Matches are head-to-head between two bots.
4.3 Three hole cards. Each player is dealt 3 hole cards instead of 2.

4.4 Simultaneous exposed discard. After the flop betting round, if no player has folded,
each player secretly chooses one hole card to discard. Both choices are made simultaneously.
Both discarded cards are then revealed to both players and removed from play. This also
happens when a player is all-in.
4.5 At showdown, each player makes the best five-card hand from their remaining 2 hole cards
and the 5 board cards, using standard hand rankings.
4.6 Betting. No-limit. Small blind 1, big blind 2. Each player starts every hand with 200
chips (stacks reset every hand). The button posts the small blind, acts first preflop, and
acts second on later streets. Seats alternate every hand.
4.7 The minimum raise is the size of the previous bet or raise, and never less than 2 chips.
There are at most 4 bets or raises per betting round, with the big blind counting as the
first on preflop.
5 Bot Requirements
5.1 Bots must be written in Python and run on Python 3.11.
5.2 Only the standard library, numpy and scipy may be used. No packages may be installed at
runtime.
5.3 Each bot runs with 1 vCPU and 1 GB RAM. (provisional)
5.4 Bots have no network access and run on a read-only filesystem. A bot may not read or
write files, open sockets, start other processes, or attempt to leave its sandbox.
5.5 Each bot has a cumulative time bank per match, which is not reset between hands: 60
seconds for a Stage 1 match of 400 hands, and 150 seconds for a Stage 2 match of about
1000 hands. (provisional)
5.6 Any single response must arrive within 5 seconds. A hard wall-clock kill applies to each
match: 180 seconds in Stage 1 and 450 seconds in Stage 2. (provisional)
5.7 A bot has 5 seconds from launch to be ready for the first message. This time is not charged
to the time bank.
5.8 Bots communicate only through the stdin/stdout protocol in the Protocol Specification.
Output on stdout must contain only protocol messages. Output on stderr is captured,
limited to 64 KB per match, and never parsed.
5.9 A bot keeps no state between matches and is started fresh for each match. Randomness is
allowed.
5.10 A bot’s source must be a single bot.py, or a .zip file with bot.py at its top level, no larger
than 5 MB.
5.11 All limits apply identically to every bot, and all matches in a round run on equal machines.
6 Submission
6.1 Each team submits one final bot (the submissions are to be made on unstop itself ).
6.2 The submission deadline is 15 November 2026 at 23:59 IST, at which point code is
frozen. Uploads after this time are not accepted.
6.3 A team with no valid upload at the deadline is not entered into the tournament.
6.4 If a fault in the organisers’ upload portal prevents uploads, the organisers will extend the
deadline for all teams and announce it.

7 Tournament Structure
7.1 Every deal is played twice, with the cards swapped between seats, so that each duplicate
deal is two hands. All results are computed over duplicate deals.
7.2 Stage 1 (Swiss qualifier). All valid bots play 6 rounds, or 7 to 8 rounds if more than
about 1000 teams submit. Each round is a match of 200 duplicate deals (400 hands).
7.3 Round 1 pairings are random. In later rounds, teams are ranked by match points, then by
capped chips per hand (Section 8), then randomly, and are paired with the closest-ranked
team they have not yet played, within the same match-point group where possible. No two
teams are paired more than once.
7.4 A bye is awarded when the number of teams is odd. It goes to the lowest-ranked team that
has not yet had one, and counts as a win. A bye adds nothing to the capped score.
7.5 If fewer than about 64 bots submit, Stage 1 is a full round robin instead of a Swiss. If 32
or fewer valid bots submit, Stage 1 is skipped.
7.6 The top 32 teams after Stage 1 advance to Stage 2. Ties at the cut-off are resolved by
Section 8.
7.7 Stage 2 (finals). The top 32 play a full round robin of 322 = 496 matches, each of 500
duplicate deals (about 1000 hands).
7.8 Scores are reset before Stage 2. The Stage 2 standings alone determine the final result and
the prizes.
7.9 Deals are generated from secret random seeds, which are published after the results.
8 Scoring and Tie-Breaks
8.1 The bot with the greater net chips over a match wins it. Equal net chips is a draw.
8.2 Match points are: win 1, draw 0.5, loss 0. Teams are ranked by total match points.

8.4 Teams still level after 8.3, for a prize position or the Stage 2 cut-off, play a replay: a match

seed.
8.5 Teams level for a position that carries no prize and no qualification effect share that position.
9 Crashes, Timeouts and Illegal Actions
9.1 A bot that crashes, exhausts its time bank, exceeds the per-response limit, or is stopped by
the wall-clock kill forfeits the remainder of the match.
9.2 A forfeited match is a loss for that bot, and the opponent’s result for that match is set to
∆ = +C. If both bots forfeit in the same hand, the match is a draw with ∆ = 0 for both.

9.3 An action that is not legal, or a message that cannot be parsed, is corrected to a check
where checking is legal, and to a fold otherwise.
9.4 An illegal or missing discard is corrected to discarding the lowest-ranked of the three hole
cards (2 is lowest, ace is highest). Among cards of equal rank, the one whose suit comes
first in the order spades, hearts, diamonds, clubs is discarded.
9.5 If a match is disrupted by a fault of the organisers’ engine or hardware, it is replayed in full
and the original result is void.
10 Conduct
10.1 A bot must be the team’s own work. Copying code from other teams or from public
bot repositories is prohibited, except for material supplied by the organisers. Standard
libraries and published algorithms may be used.
10.2 Collusion between teams, including signalling between bots, sharing code or strategy, and
deliberately losing to benefit another team, is prohibited.
10.3 Attempting to access the network, interfere with the server or other bots, or exploit the
evaluation system is prohibited. A team that finds a weakness must report it to the
organisers and not use it.
10.4 The code of prize-winning teams is reviewed before prizes are awarded. The organisers
may review any team’s code and run similarity checks across submissions.
10.5 A team found in breach of these rules is disqualified, loses any prize, and its results are
removed.
10.6 The organisers’ decision on the interpretation of these rules and on any dispute is final.
