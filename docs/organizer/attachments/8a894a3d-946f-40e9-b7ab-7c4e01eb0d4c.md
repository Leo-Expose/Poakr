Problem Statement
Discard Hold’em
Released: 1 November 2026 · Submission deadline: 15 November 2026, 23:59 IST
This Problem Statement is part of the Pokerbots Rulebook. It gives the complete rules of Discard
Hold’em with worked examples. The message interface is defined in the Protocol Specification; eli-
gibility, tournament structure and scoring are defined in the Rulebook. Values marked (provisional)
may be adjusted after benchmarking. Corrections or clarifications are announced to all teams.
1 The Task
Write a bot in Python 3.11 that plays Discard Hold’em, a heads-up (two-player) no-limit Texas
Hold’em variant, against other teams’ bots. Your bot plays matches of several hundred hands,
communicating with the organisers’ engine through stdin and stdout. The bot that wins more chips
over a match wins that match.
Discard Hold’em differs from standard Texas Hold’em in exactly two ways:
1. Three hole cards. Each player is dealt 3 hole cards instead of 2.
2. Simultaneous exposed discard. After the flop betting round, if no player has folded, each
player secretly chooses one of their hole cards to discard. Both choices are made at the same
time, then both discarded cards are revealed to both players and removed from play. Each
player plays the rest of the hand with 2 hole cards.

2 Cards
2.1 Deck and notation
A standard 52-card deck is used: 13 ranks (2 3 4 5 6 7 8 9 T J Q K A) in 4 suits (spades s,
hearts h, diamonds d, clubs c). In the protocol a card is a two-character string such as As, Td or 2c.
In this document the same cards are printed as A♠, T♢, 2♣. The ace is the highest rank, except
in the straight A–2–3–4–5 (Section 6).
2.2 The deal
For each deal the engine shuffles a full deck using a secret random seed (Rulebook 7.9) and fixes,
before any action:
• 3 hole cards for each seat, and

• the 5 board cards (3 flop, 1 turn, 1 river).
The board does not depend on any player’s action or discard. A discarded card was in a player’s
hand, so it could never have appeared on the board.
2.3 Duplicate deals
Every deal is played twice, with the cards swapped between seats (Rulebook 7.1): the bot that held
one set of hole cards in the first hand holds the other set in the second, against the same board.
Bots are not told which hands are duplicates of each other. Results are computed over duplicate
deals, so the luck of the cards largely cancels out.
3 Structure of a Hand
1. Blinds. The button posts the small blind (1); the other player posts the big blind (2). Seats
alternate every hand.
2. Deal. Each player receives 3 hole cards, face down.
3. Preflop betting. The button acts first.
4. Flop. Three board cards are revealed.
5. Flop betting. The big blind acts first.
6. Discard. If no player has folded, both players discard one hole card simultaneously; both
discards are revealed (Section 5).
7. Turn. One board card is revealed, followed by betting; the big blind acts first.
8. River. One board card is revealed, followed by betting; the big blind acts first.
9. Showdown. If no player has folded, the better hand wins the pot.
If a player folds at any point, the hand ends immediately and the other player wins the pot. No
further cards are revealed. If the fold happens before the flop betting ends, there is no discard.
4 Betting
4.1 Stacks and blinds
Each player starts every hand with 200 chips; stacks do not carry over between hands. The blinds
count as chips committed on the preflop street.
4.2 Order of action
Preflop, the button (small blind) acts first and the big blind acts second. On the flop, turn and
river, the big blind acts first and the button acts second.

4.3 Actions
Action When it is legal and what it does
fold Only when facing a bet (to_call > 0). Gives up the hand and the pot.
check Only when not facing a bet (to_call = 0). Passes without committing chips.
call Only when facing a bet. Commits enough chips to match the opponent’s bet.
raise Opens the betting or raises an existing bet, to a total amount between min_raise and
max_raise. An opening bet is also sent as raise.
4.4 “To” amounts
All raise amounts are totals for the current street, not increments. If you have committed 2 on this
street and raise to 10, you add 8 chips, and your street total becomes 10.
4.5 Minimum raise
The minimum raise-to amount is
min_raise = opp_bet + max(s, 2),
where s is the size of the last bet or raise on this street. If nobody has bet on the street, the
minimum opening bet is 2. Preflop, the big blind counts as the first raise (s = 2), so the first
preflop minimum is 2 + 2 = 4.
For example, if the button raises preflop from 2 to 6, the raise size is s = 4, and the big blind’s
minimum re-raise is to 6 + 4 = 10.
4.6 Maximum raise and all-in
The maximum raise-to amount is the all-in amount, the most a player can have committed on the
street. Both players start each hand with 200 chips, and every completed street leaves both players
equally committed, so the all-in amount is always the same for both players. If the all-in amount
is below the usual minimum raise, the all-in is still legal: min_raise = max_raise, and only that
amount may be raised to. A player facing an all-in can only call or fold.
4.7 Raise cap
At most 4 bets or raises are allowed per betting round. Preflop, the big blind counts as the first, so
at most 3 voluntary raises can follow it. On later streets the opening bet counts as the first. When
the cap is reached, raise is no longer legal and the remaining player may only call or fold.
4.8 End of a betting round
A betting round ends when both players have acted and have committed equal amounts on the
street, or when a player folds. In particular:
• If both players check, the round ends.
• Preflop, if the button only calls the big blind, the big blind still has the option to check (ending
the round) or raise.
• A call of a bet or raise ends the round, except the button’s preflop call of the big blind, after
which the big blind still has its option.

4.9 All-in hands
Once a player is all-in and the other has called, no further betting takes place. The remaining board
cards are revealed, the discard still happens after the flop, and the hand goes to showdown. No
action requests are sent for streets with no betting.
5 The Discard
• When. After the flop betting round ends, if no player has folded. This includes hands in
which one or both players are all-in.
• Simultaneous. Both players receive the discard request at the same time. Neither player
sees the other’s choice before committing to their own.
• Exposed. After both discards are in, both discarded cards are revealed to both players. Each
player’s remaining 2 hole cards stay hidden until showdown.
• Removed from play. A discarded card cannot be used at showdown. The board is already
fixed, so discards do not change the turn or river cards.
• Default discard. If a bot sends an illegal, missing or unparsable discard, the engine discards
the lowest-ranked of its three hole cards (2 lowest, ace highest). Among cards of equal rank,
the one whose suit comes first in the order spades, hearts, diamonds, clubs is discarded.
The discard is a strategic decision. The best two cards to keep depend on the flop (pairs, straight
and flush draws, kickers). Your own discard is public, and it tells the opponent something about
what you chose to keep.
6 Showdown
At showdown each player makes the best possible five-card hand from any combination of their 2
remaining hole cards and the 5 board cards, using both, one or none of their hole cards. A player
whose best five cards are the board itself is said to “play the board”.
6.1 Hand rankings
From highest to lowest:
Hand Description Example
1 Straight flush Five consecutive ranks, same suit 9♡ 8♡ 7♡ 6♡ 5♡
2 Four of a kind Four cards of one rank Q♠ Q♡ Q♢ Q♣ 4♠
3 Full house Three of one rank and two of another 7♠ 7♡ 7♢ K♣ K♠
4 Flush Five cards of the same suit A♢ J♢ 8♢ 6♢ 2♢
5 Straight Five consecutive ranks T♠ 9♢ 8♡ 7♣ 6♠
6 Three of a kind Three cards of one rank 5♠ 5♡ 5♣ K♢ 9♠

The ace may be used low only in the straight A♠ 2♢ 3♡ 4♣ 5♠ (the lowest straight). Straights do
not wrap around: Q–K–A–2–3 is not a straight.

6.2 Ties
Hands of the same category are compared by the ranks that make them, then by kickers, in the usual
way (for example, two players with a pair of aces are compared by their three highest remaining
cards in order). Suits never break ties. If both best hands are equal, the pot is split. At showdown
both players have always committed the same number of chips, so a split pot returns each player’s
chips exactly and both players’ net result is 0.
7 Chips, Matches and Scoring
• Net chips per hand. The winner of a hand wins the chips the loser committed. After a
fold, the folding player loses exactly what they had committed. After a showdown, the loser
loses their full commitment. A split pot gives net 0.
• Match. A match is a series of duplicate deals between two bots: 200 deals (400 hands) in
Stage 1 and 500 deals (about 1000 hands) in Stage 2.
• Winning a match. The bot with greater net chips over the match wins. Equal net chips is
a draw. Match points: win 1, draw 0.5, loss 0.
• Tie-break. Teams level on match points are ranked by capped chips per hand:

8.5 Example 5: Kickers and a split pot
Kicker. The board is A♡ K♢ 8♣ 5♠ 2♡. A keeps A♣ Q♠ and B keeps A♢ J♠. Both have a pair of
aces with a king. A’s hand (A–A–K–Q–8) beats B’s (A–A–K–J–8) on the fourth card.
Split pot. The board is T♠ J♠ Q♢ K♡ A♣, an ace-high straight. A keeps 9♣ 9♢ and B keeps
4♣ 4♡. Neither pair improves on the straight, so both players play the board. The pot is split and
both players’ net is 0.
8.6 Example 6: Corrections by the engine
• Illegal discard. A bot holding 7♢ 7♠ A♣ replies {"type":"discard","card":"Kh"}. K♡
is not one of its cards, so the engine discards the lowest rank. Both sevens qualify, and spades
comes before diamonds, so 7♠ is discarded. The bot receives:
{"type":"notice","code":"illegal_discard","applied":"7s"}
• Illegal raise. With to_call = 0 and min_raise = 2, a bot replies with a raise to 1. Checking
is legal, so the action is corrected to check.
• Illegal check. Facing a bet, a bot replies check. Checking is not legal, so the action is
corrected to fold.
• Stray output. A debug print on stdout is a malformed message, and it is corrected in the
same way as an illegal action. Use stderr for debugging.
9 What Your Bot Must Do
• Read engine messages from stdin, one JSON object per line, and answer every action_request
and discard_request with exactly one flushed line on stdout.
• Ignore message types and fields it does not recognise.
• Choose legal actions and legal raise amounts, and choose which hole card to discard.
• Stay within the time limits for every match, never crash, and exit after match_end.
